# IC开放题／创意题：67道答案

## 使用说明

本资料只覆盖附件第6部分“开放题（创意题，1分钟）”O1—O67，保留原题顺序；附件中的备考建议作为背景，不替代你的写作要求。

每题英文正文均为100词（按空格分隔计数，连字符词和缩写各算一个词）；题目、提示和中文翻译不计入词数。不同软件的计词规则可能略有不同。

每题附完整中文翻译，不是记忆提纲。翻译以自然、忠实表达为目标，不机械逐词对应。

一分钟能否讲完取决于语速、停顿和题目难度，建议逐题录音计时。保留自己的语气，并允许实际作答比文字更简洁。

个人事实示范：O45酵种、O50收藏、O51玩具、O54做饭、O59家、O65笔记本均为拟议内容，不代表你真实拥有或经历。请改成真实素材，勿把示范照搬成个人事实。O30文化背景及其他偏好题也应与你本人一致。

比较题使用不同判断标准，三用法题明确给出三项，脑洞题先回应再展开。幽默、观察、解释和价值判断分布在不同题目中，不要求每题都升华成大道理。

数量判断和宏观健康题不编造统计；荒岛、工具和宠物等题保留必要条件，作为面试思考练习，不视为实际生存或专业操作指南。

## O01. 头发 vs 皮肤

*Compare hair and skin.*

**English · 100 words**

Hair and skin both tell stories about us, but they communicate in different ways. Hair is almost like an editable headline: we can cut it, colour it, or change its shape to express a mood. Skin is closer to a living archive, carrying signs of age, sunlight, and sometimes experience. Both also protect us, although skin performs a much broader biological role. What interests me is how society judges these visible features before knowing the person. I would enjoy experimenting with hair, while being careful about those judgements. Appearance introduces us, while leaving room for the stories we choose ourselves.

**完整中文翻译**

头发和皮肤都能讲述关于我们的故事，但表达方式不同。头发像可以修改的标题：我们能剪发、染发或改变造型来表达心情。皮肤则更像一本活的档案，留下年龄、阳光，有时还有经历的痕迹。两者也保护我们，不过皮肤承担的生物功能广泛得多。我感兴趣的是，社会常在了解一个人之前，就根据这些可见特征作出判断。我愿意尝试不同发型，同时警惕这些判断。外貌介绍我们，同时为我们自己选择讲述的故事留出空间。


## O02. 汽车 vs 火车

*Compare a car and a train.*

**English · 100 words**

A car gives you control over a journey; a train asks you to join a journey shared with others. By car, I can change the route or stop somewhere unexpected. On a train, someone else handles the driving, giving me time to read, observe, or think. Their usefulness depends on the setting: a rural family trip may favour a car, while travel between well-connected cities may favour rail. I also like how trains create small, temporary communities among strangers. My ideal transport system preserves personal flexibility while making shared journeys convenient enough to become an attractive choice for more people.

**完整中文翻译**

汽车让你掌控旅程，火车则让你加入与他人共享的旅程。开车时，我能改变路线，或在意想不到的地方停下。坐火车时，驾驶由别人负责，我便有时间阅读、观察或思考。它们是否有用取决于情境：乡村家庭出游可能更适合汽车，交通连接良好的城市之间旅行可能更适合铁路。我也喜欢火车在陌生人之间形成短暂的小群体。理想交通系统保留个人灵活性，同时让共享旅程足够方便，成为更多人的有吸引力的选择。


## O03. 汽车 vs 自行车（compare bike and car）

*Compare a car and a bicycle.*

**English · 100 words**

For a short urban journey, I would usually choose a bicycle because it changes how I experience the city. I notice the slope of a street, the smell of a bakery, and the distance my own effort can cover. A car provides weather protection, carrying capacity, and accessibility for journeys cycling cannot reasonably serve. However, when everyone chooses a car for convenience, congestion can reduce that convenience collectively. The bicycle therefore raises a useful question: how much vehicle does this journey actually require? I would choose according to distance, safety, ability, and luggage, rather than declare one option universally better.

**完整中文翻译**

短途城市出行时，我通常会选自行车，因为它改变了我体验城市的方式。我会注意街道的坡度、面包店的香气，以及自己用力能骑多远。汽车提供遮风挡雨、载物能力，也方便完成不适合骑车的行程。不过，当所有人都为了方便选择汽车时，拥堵可能削弱大家的方便程度。我会根据距离、安全、能力和行李选择，而不宣布某一种普遍更好。


## O04. 船 vs 飞机

*Compare a boat and a plane.*

**English · 100 words**

A plane compresses distance, while a boat makes distance part of the experience. Flying is valuable when the destination matters more than the journey, especially across continents. Travelling by boat can make the landscape feel continuous: you watch the shore recede rather than suddenly appear above another city. They also face different constraints, including infrastructure, weather, and the amount they can transport. If I were travelling urgently, I would choose the plane. If I had time and a suitable route, I would be curious about the boat. For me, efficient travel serves the journey's purpose, which speed alone cannot define.

**完整中文翻译**

飞机压缩了距离，船则让距离成为体验的一部分。尤其跨洲旅行时，如果目的地比旅途更重要，飞行很有价值。乘船让景观显得连续：你看着岸边慢慢远去，而不是突然出现在另一座城市上空。两者也面临不同限制，包括基础设施、天气和运输量。若有急事，我会选飞机；若时间充裕、路线合适，我会对乘船感兴趣。对我而言，高效旅行服务于旅程目的，而速度本身无法定义这个目的。


## O05. 船 vs 汽车

*Compare a boat and a car.*

**English · 100 words**

A boat and a car both extend human movement, but they reveal how much freedom depends on the environment. A car can turn at the next junction, provided a road exists. A boat can seem freer on open water, yet weather, depth, and safe landing points shape its possibilities. I like this apparent contradiction: open space does not always mean unrestricted choice. For everyday errands, the car is usually more practical where roads connect the places I need. For crossing water or experiencing a coastline, the boat becomes essential. I would compare the obstacles each solves before imagining unrestricted freedom.

**完整中文翻译**

船和汽车都扩展了人的移动能力，但也说明自由多么依赖环境。只要有路，汽车能在下一个路口转弯。船在开阔水面上似乎更自由，但天气、水深和安全靠岸点决定了它的可能性。我喜欢这个表面矛盾：空间开阔并不总意味着选择没有限制。若日常办事的地点都有道路连接，汽车通常更实用；若要渡水或体验海岸线，船就不可或缺。在想象不受限制的自由之前，我会比较两者各自解决的障碍。


## O06. 水果 vs 蔬菜

*Compare fruit and vegetables.*

**English · 100 words**

Fruit and vegetables show how the same object can belong to different categories depending on the question. Botanically, a tomato is a fruit, but in a kitchen we usually treat it as a vegetable because of how we cook and serve it. Neither perspective is useless; each organises knowledge for a different purpose. Nutritionally, I would focus more on variety and preparation than on making one category the winner. What I find interesting is that everyday labels feel obvious until a boundary case appears. The tomato reminds me to ask what categories explain before insisting on one supposedly correct label.

**完整中文翻译**

水果和蔬菜说明，同一物品会因问题不同而被划入不同类别。植物学上，番茄是果实；在厨房中，我们通常根据烹调和食用方式把它当成蔬菜。两种视角都有用，因为它们服务于不同目的。从营养角度，我更关注种类多样性和烹调方式，而不是让某一类别胜出。我觉得有趣的是，日常标签看起来理所当然，直到边界案例出现。番茄提醒我，坚持一个所谓正确标签之前，要先问分类解释了什么。


## O07. 门 vs 窗户

*Compare a door and a window.*

**English · 100 words**

A door manages participation; a window manages perspective. Through a window, I can observe a world without entering it. Through a door, I cross a boundary and become involved. Both connect spaces while preserving separation, but they offer different degrees of access. I would choose a window when I need light or time to understand a situation, and a door when I am ready to act. There is also an important social dimension: a welcoming building needs entrances people can actually use, not just attractive views. It reminds me that seeing an opportunity and accessing it are two different experiences.

**完整中文翻译**

门管理参与，窗管理视角。透过窗，我能观察一个世界而不进入其中；穿过门，我越过边界并参与其中。两者既连接空间，也保留区隔，但提供的进入程度不同。需要光线或时间理解情境时，我会选择窗；准备行动时，我会选择门。这里也有重要的社会层面：一座友好的建筑需要人们真正能使用的入口，而不只是好看的风景。它提醒我，看见机会与获得机会是两种不同体验。


## O08. 眼睛 vs 嘴巴

*Compare eyes and mouths.*

**English · 100 words**

Eyes and mouths create a loop between understanding and expression. Our eyes gather clues, while our mouths allow us to ask, explain, and respond. Yet seeing something does not guarantee understanding it, and speaking confidently does not guarantee being right. In a difficult conversation, I would use my eyes to notice expressions and my mouth to check my interpretation rather than announce it as fact. Both also bring ordinary pleasures: watching a sunset, tasting food, or sharing laughter. I would hesitate to rank them because they support different parts of connection. I value cooperation between observation and expression across differences.

**完整中文翻译**

眼睛和嘴巴在理解与表达之间形成循环。眼睛收集线索，嘴巴帮助我们提问、解释和回应。然而，看见不保证理解，自信地说话也不保证正确。在困难的对话中，我会用眼睛观察表情，再用嘴巴核对自己的理解，而不是直接把解释当事实宣布。它们也带来日常快乐，比如看日落、品尝食物和一起大笑。我不愿给它们排名，因为它们支持着连接中的不同部分。我珍视在差异之中观察与表达的配合。


## O09. 手 vs 脚

*Compare hands and feet.*

**English · 100 words**

Hands help us shape the world, while feet help us find our place within it. A hand can make a tiny adjustment, write a sentence, or offer reassurance through a gentle gesture. Feet carry us towards people and experiences, often doing essential work we notice only when walking becomes difficult. Their relationship resembles exploration and creation: movement brings us somewhere, and manipulation lets us do something there. I would not assume everyone's body works in the same way; tools and accessible design can support both functions differently. Their cooperation supports independence through movements we rarely stop to notice or appreciate.

**完整中文翻译**

手帮助我们塑造世界，脚帮助我们在世界中找到位置。手能进行细微调整、写下一句话，或用轻柔的动作安慰他人。脚带我们走向人和经历，承担的必要工作常在行走困难时才被注意到。两者像探索与创造的关系：移动让我们到达，操作让我们在那里做事。我不会假设每个人的身体功能都相同，工具和无障碍设计能以不同方式支持这两种功能。两者配合，通过我们鲜少停下来留意或欣赏的动作支持独立生活。


## O10. 海洋 vs 湖泊

*Compare an ocean and a lake.*

**English · 100 words**

The ocean makes me think about scale and connection; a lake makes me think about attention and boundaries. Oceans connect distant places through currents and trade, while a lake can reveal a local landscape in unusually concentrated detail. Standing beside either, I might feel calm, but their ecosystems and risks are not interchangeable. A quiet surface does not tell us how healthy the water is. I would enjoy the ocean when I want a sense of possibility and a lake when I want to notice what is nearby. Their surfaces conceal complex relationships that deserve attention beyond what we see.

**完整中文翻译**

海洋让我想到尺度与连接，湖泊让我想到专注与边界。海洋通过洋流和贸易连接遥远地区，湖泊则能集中呈现一处地方的景观。站在它们旁边，我都可能感到平静，但两者的生态系统和风险不能混为一谈。安静的水面不能说明水体有多健康。想感受可能性时，我会喜欢海洋；想关注身边事物时，我会喜欢湖泊。它们表面掩藏着复杂关系，值得我们关注可见部分以外的内容。


## O11. 草 vs 沙子

*Compare grass and sand.*

**English · 100 words**

Grass and sand produce very different relationships with a landscape. Grass is living growth: it changes with seasons and depends on water, soil, and care. Sand consists of small particles shaped and moved by forces such as wind and water. Yet neither is merely a surface. A lawn may support some uses while requiring substantial maintenance, and a sandy environment can contain specialised life we overlook. Personally, grass suggests a place to pause; sand suggests movement, especially when footprints disappear. I find that disappearance interesting because it makes a visit feel temporary. Landscapes deserve appreciation without automatically serving my convenience.

**完整中文翻译**

草与沙让人与景观建立不同关系。草是有生命的生长，会随季节变化，依赖水、土壤和照料。沙由小颗粒组成，受到风和水等力量塑造与搬运。但它们都不只是地表。草坪方便一些用途，却可能需要大量维护；沙地也可能存在我们忽视的特殊生命。对我而言，草意味着停留，沙意味着变化，尤其当脚印消失时。我觉得这种消失很有趣，因为它让来访显得短暂。景观值得欣赏，而不必自动服务我的方便。


## O12. 城市 vs 村庄

*Compare a city and a village.*

**English · 100 words**

A city offers density of possibility: more people, services, and unexpected encounters nearby. A village can offer continuity, where relationships develop through repeated contact and local knowledge. Neither guarantees belonging. A city can feel lonely, and a village can feel restrictive to someone who does not fit its expectations. I would prefer a city at a stage when I want to explore careers and cultures, while preserving some village-like habits, such as knowing my neighbours. The real choice is less about population size than the combination of opportunity, privacy, and connection. I want somewhere I can both grow and contribute.

**完整中文翻译**

城市提供密集的可能性：在附近，有更多人、服务和意外相遇。村庄提供延续感，人际关系通过反复接触和本地知识发展。两者都不保证归属感：城市可能孤独，村庄也可能让不符合期待的人感到受限。在想探索职业和文化的阶段，我会更愿意住城市，同时保留一些村庄式习惯，比如认识邻居。真正的选择与其说是人口多少，不如说是机会、隐私和连接的组合。我想住在既能成长、也能贡献的地方。


## O13. 有线充电器 vs 无线充电器

*Compare wired and wireless chargers.*

**English · 100 words**

A wired charger makes the connection visible; a wireless charger makes it more convenient in some everyday situations. With a cable, I can often keep using the phone while charging and understand immediately whether it is connected. A charging pad reduces repeated plugging, but positioning, compatibility, and energy losses can affect the experience. I would choose a cable for reliability while travelling and consider a pad for a stable desk routine. What interests me is the difference between removing a visible action and removing effort overall. convenience reduces uncertainty and interruption, rather than merely hiding the equipment doing the work.

**完整中文翻译**

有线充电器让连接可见，无线充电器则在一些日常情境中更方便。使用电线时，我通常能边充边用，也能立即知道是否连接。充电板减少了反复插拔，但放置位置、兼容性和能量损失都会影响体验。旅行时我会为可靠性选择电线；固定书桌上则可以考虑充电板。我感兴趣的是，去掉一个看得见的动作，与减少整体努力并不相同。便利减少不确定性和打断，而不只是藏起完成工作的设备。


## O14. 世界上窗户更多还是树更多

*Are there more windows or trees in the world?*

**English · 100 words**

My guess is that there are more trees than windows worldwide, but I would explain the estimate rather than pretend certainty. First, I would clarify whether we count building windows, vehicle windows, and small saplings. Then I would estimate windows from the number of buildings and their average window counts, separating homes from large commercial buildings. For trees, I would use land area and approximate density across forests and other habitats. The result could change with definitions, but trees seem likely to dominate globally. The useful answer exposes assumptions and possible reversals, instead of offering a precise but unsupported number.

**完整中文翻译**

我猜全球树比窗户多，但会解释估算，而不会假装确定。首先要明确是否计入建筑窗、车窗和小树苗。然后根据建筑数量和平均窗户数量估算窗户，并区分住宅与大型商业建筑。树则可以根据森林等生境的面积与大致密度估算。定义改变可能改变结果，不过全球范围内树很可能占优势。有用的回答展示假设及可能的反转，而不提供精确却无根据的数字。


## O15. 世界上人更多还是车更多

*Are there more people or cars in the world?*

**English · 100 words**

I would expect people to outnumber cars globally. The simplest reason is that car ownership is uneven: many people are children, cannot afford a car, or live where other transport is more practical. Some households share one vehicle, although businesses and wealthy owners may have several. To test the judgement, I would compare population estimates with registered passenger vehicles, then check whether the question includes trucks and unregistered cars. Definitions matter, but I would still expect people to remain ahead. The interesting follow-up is whether more cars would represent progress. I judge transport by useful access, rather than vehicle ownership.

**完整中文翻译**

我预计全球人数多于汽车数。最简单的理由是，汽车拥有情况并不均衡：许多人是儿童、买不起车，或住在其他交通方式更实用的地方。有些家庭共用一辆车，虽然企业和富裕车主可能有多辆。要检验判断，我会比较人口估计与登记乘用车数量，再明确是否包括卡车和未登记汽车。定义很重要，但我仍预计人数领先。有趣的后续问题是，车更多是否代表进步。我根据有效通达性评价交通，而不是车辆拥有情况。


## O16. 香蕉和黄瓜打架谁会赢

*Who would win a fight between a banana and a cucumber?*

**English · 100 words**

I would back the cucumber in a wrestling match because it seems sturdier, but the banana would win a popularity contest. The question becomes much more interesting once we define what winning means. If the challenge involved making someone laugh, the banana's curved shape and familiar comic associations would give it an advantage. If both could negotiate, I would suggest abandoning the fight and opening a smoothie stand, although the recipe would need testing. My answer is playful, but the reasoning is serious: strengths depend on the rules and the objective. I would first ask whether cooperation could outperform competition.

**完整中文翻译**

摔跤赛中我会支持黄瓜，因为它看起来更结实；人气赛中香蕉会赢。一旦明确赢意味着什么，这题就更有意思。如果挑战是逗人笑，香蕉的弯曲外形和常见喜剧联想会让它占优。如果它们能谈判，我会建议停止打架，合开一家果昔店，不过配方还需要测试。答案很轻松，但推理是认真的：优势取决于规则和目标。我会先问合作能否比竞争创造更好的结果。


## O17. 纸（Paper）的三种用法

*Give three uses for paper.*

**English · 100 words**

I would use paper in three ways: to think, to test, and to connect. First, sketching an idea on paper helps me see relationships without worrying about making the result polished. Second, folding paper into a simple model can test a package or explain a spatial problem before expensive production. Third, a handwritten message can make an ordinary expression of thanks feel personal. These uses rely on different qualities: flexibility, physical form, and the trace of someone's effort. I would reuse scrap paper where possible. I appreciate how inexpensive paper supports experiments and personal attention that digital speed can obscure.

**完整中文翻译**

我会用纸来思考、测试和连接。首先，在纸上画想法能帮助我看清关系，不必担心成品是否精美。其次，将纸折成简单模型，可以在昂贵制作前测试包装或解释空间问题。第三，手写留言能让普通的感谢更有个人温度。这些用途依赖不同特性：灵活性、实体形态，以及某人付出努力的痕迹。我会尽量重复使用废纸。我欣赏便宜的纸既支持实验，也承载数字速度可能遮蔽的个人专注。


## O18. 铅笔（Pencil）的三种用法

*Give three uses for a pencil.*

**English · 100 words**

A pencil can be a writing tool, a measuring reference, and a small garden marker. Writing is the familiar use, and erasable marks are useful for exploring ideas. I could use its known length to estimate the size of an object when no ruler is available. Finally, an old pencil placed beside a seedling could support a labelled paper flag identifying the plant. These are modest uses, but they show how creativity starts by noticing properties: a pencil leaves marks, has a physical length, and can stand upright in soil.
